# On the local geometry of the tight 39-point code in 𝕆P²

Cohn, Kumar and Minton constructed a universally optimal code of thirty-nine points in the octonionic projective plane 𝕆P², whose existence was conjectured by Hoggar. Its points form thirteen mutually unbiased Jordan frames, and a four-parameter phase family deforms the code through such configurations. This Lean development describes their local geometry under the automorphism group F₄. At every nonresonant phase, every nearby real solution of the defining equations belongs to one of four smooth families of labelled configurations. In a local slice for F₄, their union has normal form R⁴ × ⋃ᵢ₌₁⁴ R⁶ᵢ, with the R⁶ᵢ in direct sum. The distance to the localized family union is bounded by a phase-dependent constant times the square root of the residual, and the exponent 1/2 is sharp in the ambient configuration space. On an explicit open dense subset of the nonresonant phases, the complexified families are exactly the top-dimensional components of the complex solution germ, each generically reduced. Every neighbourhood of the original code in the space of unlabelled tight 39-point codes modulo F₄ contains points of local dimension 10.

The results are local, the resonant germ at the original code is not classified, and lower-dimensional complex components are not excluded. For exact frames and strongly convex C² overlap potentials, we prove a two-sided comparison of the energy gap with squared overlap errors and, near each nonresonant orbit, a quartic lower bound in distance to the localized family union.
